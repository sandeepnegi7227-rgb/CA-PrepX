What each question usually asks

Q1

Must attempt · small AS questions

Three or four short questions on different Accounting Standards, 4 to 5 marks each

Q2

Company accounts or reconstruction

Balance sheet as per Schedule III, or an internal reconstruction

Q3

The mixed bag

Cash flow statement, buyback or a longer AS question

Q4

Amalgamation

14 marks in 6 of 8 exams. Purchase consideration, then the new balance sheet

Q5

Consolidation

14 marks in 7 of 8 exams. Holding and subsidiary, minority interest, cost of control

Q6

Branch accounts + theory

Branch accounts for 6 marks, plus short notes and small questions

Q1 is a must. Then choose any 4 from Q2 to Q6. Each question is 14 marks.

SAFEST 28 MARKS

Master Q4 and Q5 first

Amalgamation and consolidation are long, but they come almost every exam and the format never changes.

MCQ TIP

MCQs love the small standards

Borrowing costs, deferred tax, impairment and inventories turn up as MCQs far more than as written questions.

DO NOT SKIP

Branch accounts

Only 6 marks, but it came in 7 of 8 exams. Easy marks if you know the format.

IT 3

Salaries or capital gains

A focused sum on one head of income

IT 4

Return filing + set-off

Due dates, belated and revised returns, and carrying losses forward

GST 1

Place of supply, invoices, returns, TDS and TCS, value and time of supply

How this paper behaves

1

No fixed question slots

Every question mixes three or four chapters. You cannot pick questions by chapter here

2

Internal control is the anchor

10 to 18 marks in every exam. Risk of misstatement, controls, IT environment

3

Standards on Auditing drive it

Most answers begin with what the SA says. Learn the SA number with the idea

4

Bank audit comes and goes

17 marks in one exam, zero in another. Know it, but do not bet on it

5

Audit report every exam

Opinions, emphasis of matter, CARO

6

Case-style questions

A short story, then: what should the auditor do?
